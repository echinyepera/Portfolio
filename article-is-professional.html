The Evolving Role of the IS Professional

April 15, 2025 5 min read Information Systems

Not long ago, the Information Systems department was often seen as the "tech support" corner of an organization — the team you called when your email wasn't working or when your computer froze. Today, that perception is not only outdated but dangerously wrong.

The IS professional has undergone one of the most dramatic transformations in modern business history. From maintaining servers to shaping strategy, from fixing printers to driving innovation — the evolution is nothing short of remarkable.

In this article, I reflect on this journey and what it means for aspiring IS professionals today.

A Brief History: Where We Came From

1960s-1980s

The Technical Era

IS was called "Data Processing." Professionals focused on automating manual tasks — payroll, inventory, basic record-keeping. The role was purely technical, reporting to finance or operations. Business leaders didn't understand what IS did, and frankly, didn't need to.

1990s

The Enablement Era

Enter ERP systems (SAP, Oracle). IS started enabling business processes. Professionals became project managers, implementing large-scale systems. The term "alignment" emerged — getting IS to align with business goals. But IS was still a supporter, not a driver.

2000s-2010s

The Digital Era

The internet, e-commerce, cloud computing, and data analytics changed everything. IS became central to customer experience. Amazon, Google, and Netflix showed that technology WAS the business. IS professionals started sitting at the strategy table.

2020s and Beyond

The Strategic Era

AI, machine learning, blockchain, and advanced analytics. IS is no longer just enabling strategy — IS IS the strategy. The modern IS professional is a business strategist who happens to understand technology, not the other way around.

What This Means for Today's IS Professional

The shift from "support" to "strategic driver" has fundamentally changed what organizations expect from IS professionals. Technical skills alone are no longer enough.

The Modern IS Professional Must Be:

Business Strategist
Trusted Advisor
Innovation Driver
Bridge Builder
Risk Manager
Technical Expert
Data Translator
Relationship Manager
💡 Key Shift: The question is no longer "How do we build this system?" but rather "What business value will this system create, and how does it differentiate us from competitors?"

The Three Competencies Every IS Professional Needs

1. Technical Competency (The Foundation)

You still need to understand databases, networks, systems analysis, and cybersecurity. But technical skills are now table stakes — everyone has them. What sets you apart is what you add on top.

2. Business Competency (The Differentiator)

Do you understand how your organization makes money? Can you read a balance sheet? Do you know your industry's competitive landscape? The best IS professionals speak fluent business and can translate technical possibilities into business opportunities.

3. People Competency (The Multiplier)

Can you influence without authority? Can you explain complex technical concepts to non-technical executives? Can you build relationships across departments? These "soft skills" are what turn good IS professionals into indispensable strategic partners.

Reflections from My Journey

As an Information Systems student and aspiring professional, this evolution is both exciting and challenging. It means my education must go beyond learning to code or design databases. It means I must constantly ask: "How does this technology create value?"

My final year project, AgriTrace, is a direct application of this mindset. The technical challenge (building a USSD verification system) was significant. But the real value comes from understanding the business problem — counterfeit agricultural inputs destroying farmers' livelihoods — and designing a solution that addresses it in a way that is accessible, sustainable, and impactful.

What This Means for Organizations

For business leaders, the message is clear: stop treating IS as a cost center. The organizations that thrive will be those that embed IS professionals into strategic decision-making from day one, not as an afterthought.

The most successful companies today — Amazon, Microsoft, Tesla — are technology companies at their core, regardless of their industry. Their IS professionals don't just support the business; they are the business.

📌 Takeaway for Leaders: If your IS team isn't in the room when major strategic decisions are made, you're leaving value on the table.

Conclusion: The Future Is Bright

The role of the IS professional will only become more central. As AI, automation, and data continue to reshape every industry, the ability to bridge technology and business strategy will be one of the most valuable skills in the job market.

For those of us entering this field, the opportunity is enormous. We get to be at the intersection of technology and business — building systems that don't just work, but that create competitive advantage, drive innovation, and change industries.

That's not a support role. That's a leadership role.

And that's the evolution of the IS professional.
